Two approaches to surgical visualization

Open surgery

Surgeons work directly through an incision, using familiar instruments and tactile feedback. The view depends on a direct line of sight, which can be limited in deep or narrow spaces.

Laparoscopic & robotic surgery

A camera and specialized instruments enter through small access points. Surgeons operate from a screen or robotic console, with a different setup and workflow from open surgery.

Introducing Revelascope

A video-enabled retractor blade with an integrated camera and LED illumination. Revelascope is designed to stream a magnified, shared view to an external display, with the camera moving with the blade as the dissection advances.

Revelascope is an investigational device under development. It has not been cleared or approved by the FDA and is not available for commercial use.
First application / Breast surgery

Seeing inside nipple-sparing mastectomy

Nipple-sparing mastectomy requires removing the breast gland through a small incision while preserving the skin, nipple, and areola. Surgeons work within a dark cavity with limited direct line of sight, making visualization a central challenge. Revelascope is being developed to bring the view inside that cavity while preserving an open surgical approach.

See beyond the incision

Blade-mounted imaging and illumination are designed to bring a magnified view of the dissection to an external monitor.

Keep the open surgical workflow

Revelascope is designed to preserve familiar instruments and direct tactile feedback while adding video visualization to retraction.

Give the team a shared view

An external display lets the surgical team and trainees see the operative field together, supporting communication and teaching.
